Indians actually didn’t like Maggi at first. But one smart experiment changed everything.
When Nestlé launched Maggi in 1983, no instant noodle brand existed in India. Their plan was to sell “2-minute noodles” to Indian mothers — but it flopped. Why?
Because moms preferred fresh, homemade food, not shortcuts.
Then came the breakthrough.
Adults weren’t interested, but kids loved the taste. So Maggi switched strategy. They targeted children and teenagers, ran emotional ads showing a mother cooking Maggi with love — and slowly it became the comfort food of every home.
Today, thanks to that marketing pivot, Maggi generates over ₹25,000 crore annually in India.
