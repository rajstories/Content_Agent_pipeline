# Secret Business Stories Style Guide

Use this guide for all future scripts in the same format as `script_1.md` to `script_32.md`.

## Core Format

Every script must follow this structure:

```md
Brand Name

Hinglish script

-----

English script
```

Do not use labels like `[HOOK]`, `[BODY]`, or `[CTA]` inside the final script.
The hook, body, and CTA should be naturally embedded.

## Script Length

- Hinglish version: usually 120-190 words.
- English version: usually 120-190 words.
- Total script should feel like a 35-55 second reel.
- Keep paragraphs short, but do not over-format.

## Proven Story Structure

### 1. Hook

Start with a direct shock, curiosity, or underdog line.

Common hook formulas:

- `Is [person/founder] ne [unexpected action] karke [huge brand/result] bana diya.`
- `Is [small founder] ne [big competitor] ko is tarah beat kiya.`
- `[Big brand] ne [Indian brand] ko destroy karne ki koshish ki, lekin...`
- `Jis [brand/product] ko reject kiya gaya tha, aaj woh...`
- `Is [founder] ne [waste/problem] ko use karke [crore company] bana di.`
- `Yeh [rank/hidden fact] brand hai, lekin shayad aapne naam nahi suna hoga.`

Hook should contain at least one of:
- Big number
- Famous competitor
- Founder struggle
- Controversy
- Hidden business model
- Underdog reversal
- Emotional trigger

### 2. Founder / Context

Immediately introduce the founder and the market gap.

Pattern:

`[Founder] ne notice kiya ki [market/customer problem]...`

Use concrete context:
- Year
- City
- Founder background
- Customer behavior
- Existing market problem
- Competitor weakness

### 3. Problem

The problem should be simple enough for a mass audience.

Winning problem types from existing scripts:
- Foreign brands were too expensive.
- Local products lacked quality.
- Customers had inconvenience.
- Parents/businesses had repeat cost.
- Waste was being ignored.
- Retailers rejected the idea.
- Big brands ignored small towns.
- Product was useful but distribution failed.
- Premium customers were leaving.

### 4. Smart Move

Show the founder's clever decision.

Common move types:
- Affordable version of expensive product
- Direct-to-customer model
- Better packaging
- Cleaner/hygienic positioning
- Premium/luxury repositioning
- Distribution hack
- Product innovation
- Flanker brand
- Legal/tax loophole
- Waste-to-value conversion
- Localized Indian version of foreign product

Use transition lines:
- `Isi problem ko solve karne ke liye...`
- `Tab unhone ek smart move liya...`
- `Yahin se game badla...`
- `Phir aaya turning point...`
- `Lekin story yahin khatam nahi hoti...`

### 5. Obstacle / Twist

Most strong scripts include one complication.

Examples:
- Retailers rejected it.
- Customers did not understand the product.
- Product was too expensive.
- Demand dropped.
- Competitors entered.
- People saw it as cheap.
- Distribution was hard.
- Broadcasters rejected it.

Then show the solution.

### 6. Scale Proof

End the body with proof.

Use metrics when available:
- Revenue
- Valuation
- Market share
- Stores/outlets
- Countries
- Retail network
- Celebrity investor
- Acquisition
- Daily production
- Annual sales

Tone:
- Use numbers confidently.
- Avoid over-explaining.
- Do not add fake numbers.
- If uncertain, say `around`, `nearly`, `over`, or `lagbhag`.

### 7. CTA

End with episode CTA in the same series style.

Common formats:

- `This was episode [N] of Secret business stories follow for episode [N+1]`
- `This was Episode [N] of Secret Business Stories. Follow for Episode [N+1].`
- `Share this video with [specific audience]. This was Episode [N]...`

For Hinglish, keep it natural:

- `This was episode [N] of Secret business stories follow for episode [N+1]`
- `Is video ko bhejo [audience] ke paas, this was secret business stories ka episode [N] follow for episode [N+1]`

## Language Rules

### Hinglish Voice

Use natural spoken Hinglish:

- `isliye`
- `tab`
- `lekin`
- `phir`
- `aaj`
- `dheere dheere`
- `dekhte hi dekhte`
- `yahin se game badla`
- `smart move`
- `market gap`
- `customers`
- `brand`
- `revenue`
- `valuation`
- `annual revenue`

Do not make it too polished or formal.
The style should sound like a fast business reel, not a documentary.

### English Version

The English version should mirror the Hinglish version closely.
It should sound cleaner and more fluent, but not change the facts or structure.

## Recurring High-Performing Patterns

### Underdog Founder

Use when the founder started with hardship.

Formula:
`Is [struggle/background] wale founder ne [unexpected category] me [huge company] bana di.`

### Big Brand vs Indian Brand

Use when there is a competitor angle.

Formula:
`[Big brand] ko beat karne ke liye [Indian founder] ne [smart move] kiya.`

### Hidden Business Model

Use when the story has a less-known revenue engine.

Formula:
`[Famous company] ka ek secret business hai jo [comparison] se bhi bada ban chuka hai.`

### Waste To Wealth

Use for eco/business stories.

Formula:
`Is founder ne [waste] ko use karke [premium product/company] bana diya.`

### Rejection To Success

Use when market rejected the idea first.

Formula:
`Jis [product/idea] ko pehle reject kar diya gaya tha, aaj woh [huge result] kar raha hai.`

### Smart Positioning

Use when the product succeeded because of branding.

Formula:
`Product same category me tha, lekin positioning ne game badal diya.`

## Future Script Template

```md
[Brand Name]

[HOOK: one shocking/curious line with founder/company + unusual action + big result]
[Founder/context line: founder noticed market/customer problem]
[Problem detail: why existing solution failed or why customers were frustrated]

[Obstacle/twist: why it almost failed or what challenge came]
[Solution/strategy: packaging, pricing, distribution, positioning, innovation, celebrity, etc.]
[Scale proof: revenue/valuation/market share/stores/countries/acquisition]
This was episode [N] of Secret Business Stories follow for episode [N+1]

-----

[English mirror version with same hook, same facts, same sequence]
This was Episode [N] of Secret Business Stories. Follow for Episode [N+1].
```

## Quality Checklist

Before finalizing a future script, check:

- Hook has shock/curiosity in the first line.
- The founder or company is introduced quickly.
- The problem is obvious to normal viewers.
- There is one smart business insight.
- There is at least one number or proof point.
- Story uses `lekin`, `tab`, `isliye`, or `yahin se` style transitions.
- CTA keeps the episode series format.
- English version mirrors Hinglish version.
- No unnecessary moral lesson.
- No formal intro like `Aaj main batane wala hoon`.
