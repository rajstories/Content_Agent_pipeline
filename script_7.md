KTM

An Indian company invested 7000 crores to save everyone’s favorite Austrian bike brand from Bankruptcy
In 1934, a small repair shop was started in Austria by Hans Trunkenpolz, and in 1953, Hans and his family began making their own racing motorcycles under the name KTM.
At a time when other motorcycle brands were only focused on city bikes, KTM’s focus was clear — every bike had to be race-ready straight from the factory, without any modifications.
Their racing bikes became very popular, but to expand globally, they partnered with the Indian company Bajaj Auto in 2007.
After that, bikes that used to cost 4 lakhs in Europe became available in India for just around 1.5 lakhs.
Very soon, KTM turned into a dream bike for youngsters. But after Covid, a major downfall came.
Demand dropped, but production was not reduced, and because of this, KTM fell into huge debt. That’s when Bajaj once again stepped in to help, investing 7000 crores and saving KTM from bankruptcy.
And today, a majority stake of KTM is owned by the Indian brand Bajaj.
