As kids, we all had just one question, are Natraj pencils better or Apsara pencils?
But once you know the truth, you’ll be shocked.

In 1958, BJ Sangi noticed that foreign pencil brands were too expensive for Indian consumers.
So he launched Natraj Pencils affordable, strong, and perfect for daily use.
Very quickly, Natraj became India’s best-selling pencil.

But as India’s purchasing power started increasing, foreign brands entered the market with better quality pencils in the premium segment.
Slowly, Natraj began to be seen as a reliable but cheap brand.
If things stayed the same, Natraj risked losing premium customers.

That’s when, in 1970, he quietly launched Apsara Pencils.
The idea was simple  premium customers should not move from Natraj to another company, but shift to Apsara instead.

Both brands had completely different positioning.
Natraj was for affordable, regular use  pencils that just keep going.
Apsara was positioned for exams and special occasions, with the tagline “extra marks for good handwriting.”

Soon, kids started debating Natraj vs Apsara everywhere.
What most people didn’t know was that both brands were owned by the same company  Hindustan Pencils Private Limited.

This strategy is called a flanker strategy  when a company quietly launches a second brand to protect its main brand from competition.
And today, Hindustan Pencils controls nearly 60% of the Indian pencil market all by itself.
