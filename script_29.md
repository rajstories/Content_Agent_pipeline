The Chhota Bheem that Indian broadcasters once rejected is now being shown to the entire world by Netflix, generating crores in revenue.

In the 2000s, Indian television was dominated by foreign cartoons like Shinchan and Doraemon. Around the same time, Rajiv Chilaka got the idea of creating an Indian cartoon character inspired by Bheem from the Mahabharata — a 10-year-old boy living in a village with superhuman powers.

When this idea was pitched to broadcasters, it was rejected outright. They believed Indian cartoons would never work. Still, Rajiv Chilaka didn’t give up. He invested his own savings to produce the first 13 episodes of Chhota Bheem. After multiple rejections, Pogo finally accepted the show in 2008, and Chhota Bheem quickly became a favorite among Indian children.

After its success, Netflix turned Bheem into a dialogue-free toddler show, making it perfect for global audiences.

Today, Chhota Bheem is watched in over 190 countries and generates nearly ₹120 crore in annual revenue.
