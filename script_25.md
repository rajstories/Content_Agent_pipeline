Lay’s accidentally created a desi snack that ended up eating Lay’s market itself.
Back in 1999, PepsiCo noticed people mocking Lay’s for being a “bag of air,” while Indian namkeens were cheaper — almost one-third the price. To solve this, the company experimented with alternatives to potato chips. After nearly 220 trials, they created a spicy, crunchy snack using corn and rice meal — and named it Kurkure.
But the real masterstroke was marketing. PepsiCo gave free racks to shopkeepers and hung bright orange Kurkure strings at store fronts so customers would see it first, even before Lay’s.
Slowly, Kurkure became the new favourite. People even started making homemade recipes with it.
Today Kurkure generates over ₹1000 crore annually and stands as one of India’s most loved snacks
