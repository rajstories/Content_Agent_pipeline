They sold their house in Australia to build India’s biggest art and craft brand.

Meet Harish and Rashmi, who noticed a major gap in India. Parents had no proper stores to buy good quality art and craft supplies for their kids. Seeing this opportunity, they moved back from Australia to India and in 2007 launched Itsy Bitsy in Bangalore, India’s first organized art and craft store.

But there was a big problem. People had no idea about scrapbooking or DIY crafts. Customers came, looked around, but didn’t buy anything. Instead of giving up, they started free workshops to show what could actually be created using these products. Slowly, families realized these weren’t just products, but tools for creativity.

Soon, Itsy Bitsy became the go to place for birthdays, school projects, and family bonding. They also started employing rural women to make handmade products, creating large scale impact. Today, more than 2000 women earn their livelihood through this initiative.

Now, Itsy Bitsy has 40 plus stores across India, does 70 crores in annual revenue, and has grown completely without any external funding.
